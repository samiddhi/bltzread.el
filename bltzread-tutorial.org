#+title: bltzread Tutorial
#+options: toc:t num:t author:nil date:nil

* Before you begin

This tutorial takes you from your first coloured page to a setup that suits you. Each step says what to do and what you should see. If you want to look up what an option means, the manual (=M-x bltzread-manual=) has all of them.

You need:

- Emacs with bltzread loaded (your init file already does this).
- bltzr installed, with at least one language (check with =bltzr list-languages=).
- Optional: sdcv and a StarDict dictionary, for the word cards.

* Part 1: Your first page

** Step 1. Get some text
Open any text file in the language you are learning, or copy a paragraph and run =M-x bltzread-read-text=. The second is the easiest: it opens a nicely spaced page.

** Step 2. Turn the mode on
Run =M-x bltzread-mode=. If asked, type the three-letter language code, for example =slv=, and press Enter.

You should see: after a moment, words are coloured. Strong yellow means new. Words on your skip list have no colour at all.

** Step 3. Rest on a word
Move the cursor onto a coloured word and wait half a second. A small card appears with the word, its level and, if you set up a dictionary, its meaning.

** Step 4. Mark what you know
With the cursor on a word, press =\[bltzread-set-level]= with a digit: =5= if you know it perfectly. The colour disappears. Press =1= to make it new again. Press =3= to say "familiar".

Press =\[bltzread-next-unknown]= to jump to the next word you do not know yet. Press =\[bltzread-previous-unknown]= to go back. (=s n= and =s p= do the same.) Put a count first to jump further.

A level covers every form of the word, so =3= on /hiše/ also sets /hiša/. Made a mistake? Press =\[bltzread-undo]=, or the ordinary undo key, as often as you like; redo works too.

** Step 5. Ignore a name
Put the cursor on a name and press =\[bltzread-ignore]=. It turns grey and bltzread will stop counting it. Press it again to bring the word back. If a card is in your way, press =\[bltzread-close-popup]= to close it; =\[bltzread-next-entry]= shows the next dictionary entry when there are several.

** Step 6. Look a word up properly
Press =\[bltzread-lookup]= on a word, or double-click it. A longer entry appears. Looking a word up tells bltzread you did not know it: it restarts that word's progress.

** Step 7. Leave a note
Press =\[bltzread-note]=, type your own hint, press Enter. Next time the card shows it.

** Step 8. Turn it off
Run =M-x bltzread-mode= again. The colours go and the buffer is editable.

** Jump to unknown words with avy
If you have the =avy= package, run =M-x bltzread-avy-unknown=. Every unknown word on screen gets a hint; type it to go to that word and open its card. Escape or =C-g= removes the hints and continues as =avy-goto-char-timer=. Bind it to a key you like.

* Part 2: Letting bltzread follow your reading

Now the useful part: bltzread can raise words by itself as you read.

** Step 1. Read
Turn the mode on and read a few paragraphs. Look up the words you do not know.

** Step 2. Finish the session
Press =s f=. bltzread asks which part you read. By default it proposes everything from the start to the farthest place on screen you reached. Press Enter to accept.

You should see a message saying how many words were counted. Words you did not look up now have one clean read. Do this on a few more days and they climb a level, when they have enough reads.

** Step 3. Move your bookmark
After counting, bltzread may ask whether to move the bookmark to where you stopped. Say yes to pick up there next time.

** Step 4. If you read everything easily
Press =C-u s f= to count the range and mark all words in it as known.

** Step 5. Closing the buffer
If you kill the buffer without finishing, bltzread asks whether to record your reading. Answer yes and it takes you through the same steps.

** A warning about books
In a book opened with nov.el, going to the next chapter replaces the text. Finish the session first.

* Part 3: Keeping bltzr up to date

When a word reaches level 5, bltzread queues it instead of changing your skip list straight away.

** Step 1. Open the review list
Run =M-x bltzread-review=. You see words and their basic forms. All are marked "add".

** Step 2. Decide
Move down with =n= and =p=. Press =d= for words you want to think about later and =r= to say "not really known": those drop back to level 4. Press =A= to mark all as add again.

** Step 3. Apply
Press =x=. Confirm. The words are added to your skip list; a =.bak= copy of the old list is kept beside it.

** Step 4. Check
Run bltzr on your text. The words you added no longer appear as new.

* Part 4: Basic configuration

Put settings in your init file, or use =M-x customize-group RET bltzread=.

** Make progress faster or slower
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-promotion-thresholds '(2 2 3 4))
#+end_src
The four numbers are clean reads needed to leave level 1, 2, 3 and 4.

** Change what a lookup does
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-lookup-effect 'demote)
#+end_src
A word you look up now also goes down one level.

** Change a key
The single keys work only while the buffer is read-only, so they never get in the way of typing. To move or remove one:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(keymap-unset bltzread-mode-map "N" t)
(keymap-set bltzread-mode-map "n" (bltzread-when-read-only #'bltzread-note))
#+end_src
The rarer commands follow the prefix =bltzread-prefix-key= (default =s=, so =s 3=); set it to another key in Customize, or to nil for none.

** Colours
Run =M-x customize-face RET bltzread-level-1 RET=. Change the background. Do the same for the others.

** Dictionaries
Tell bltzread where your StarDict folders are, and which to use for each language:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-sdcv-data-dir "~/archive/_goldendict")
(setq bltzread-sdcv-dictionaries
      '(("slv" "DSSJ-E-sl-en")
        ("pli" "dpd")))
#+end_src
A name may be the exact one shown by =sdcv -l -2 ~/archive/_goldendict=, the folder name, or a unique part of the name. Inside =use-package :custom= write each pair as =(bltzread-sdcv-dictionaries '(...))=, not =(setq ...)=. Languages not listed search no dictionary. If lookups return nothing, run =M-x bltzread-dict-diagnose=.

** The pasted-text page
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-paste-font-family "Georgia"
      bltzread-paste-font-height 1.5
      bltzread-paste-line-spacing 0.5
      bltzread-paste-margin 12)
#+end_src

* Part 5: Choosing how you mark what you read

Four methods exist; you will like one more than the others.

** Step 1. Try them
Run =M-x bltzread-range-playground=. It gives you some text and lets you try each method, then shows the range it chose.

** Step 2. Pick one
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-range-method 'paragraphs)
#+end_src
Choices: =farthest=, =points=, =paragraphs=, =lines=. You can switch during reading with =s m=.

** Step 3. Skip the confirmation
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-session-confirm nil)
#+end_src

* Part 6: Advanced options

** One level for all forms of a word
By default each spelling has its own level. To give a basic word and all its forms one level:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-track-by 'lemma)
#+end_src
This pairs well with writing to the word-families list:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-list-target 'word-families)
#+end_src

** Different list target per language
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-list-target 'exact-words
      bltzread-list-target-alist '(("pli" . word-families)))
#+end_src

** Skip the review
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-known-write-policy 'immediate)
#+end_src
Words reaching level 5 are written at once. Careful: you can no longer reject them. The default is =unset=: it queues words like =review= and warns once per session that you have not chosen. Choose =review= or =immediate= and the warning goes away.

** Your own skip list files
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-skip-exact-words-files
      '(("slv" . "~/lists/slv-words.txt")))
#+end_src
Otherwise bltzread reads the paths from your bltzr settings file.

** Your own dictionary
Any function that takes a language and a list of words and returns text:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(defun my-lookup (lang words)
  (shell-command-to-string
   (format "mydict %s %s" lang (car words))))
(setq bltzread-dictionary-function #'my-lookup)
#+end_src

** A language that has no spaces
Give it its own word pattern:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-word-regexp-alist '(("xyz" . "[[:word:]]+")))
#+end_src

** Count repeated words
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-count-each-occurrence t)
#+end_src
A word seen five times in the range gets five clean reads.

** The card
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-popup-trigger 'manual     ; only when you press .
      bltzread-popup-style 'side-window  ; never a floating box
      bltzread-popup-max-lines 12)
#+end_src

** Per-file language
Put this at the end of a file so bltzread knows its language:
#+begin_example
# Local Variables:
# bltzread-language: "slv"
# End:
#+end_example

* Part 7: Reading along with subtle

If you play audio or video with subtle, bltzread can credit only what was played. Once subtle is loaded, bltzread already connects itself: point tracking follows the first unknown word and opens its card (none opens when a cue has no unknown word), and the card shows the key of =subtle-point-track-toggle=. The rest is up to you. Put this in your init file:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-range-farthest-source #'subtle-played-through-position
      bltzread-range-start-function  #'subtle-played-start-position)

(add-hook 'subtle-buffer-setup-hook
          (lambda ()
            (when (= subtle-track-index 0)
              (setq bltzread-language subtle-language)
              (bltzread-mode 1))))

(add-hook 'subtle-pause-hook
          (lambda () (when bltzread-mode (bltzread-avy-unknown))))
#+end_src
Now bltzread is on for the first subtitle file only. Pause and the unknown words in view get avy hints; pick one to see its card. In a subtitle buffer the same single keys work; subtle keeps its own keys and the prefix =s=. The first load of a long transcript takes a moment while bltzr reads all its words. The manual has the details.

* Short dictionary entries

The card that appears when you rest on a word shows only the part of a dictionary entry you care about, for example the English translations. The lookup key shows the whole entry. bltzread knows nothing about any particular dictionary: you tell it, per dictionary, which pieces of the entry to show. This takes a line or two each.

** Step 1. Look at the raw entry
An entry is HTML. Print it with sdcv (=-u= takes the dictionary name from =sdcv -l -2 ~/archive/_goldendict=):
#+begin_src sh
sdcv -n -j -e -0 -1 -2 ~/archive/_goldendict -u "Polish-English" dom
#+end_src
The output is JSON; the entry is in the field =definition=. Read it for the pieces you want. In the Polish-English entry the translations look like this:
#+begin_example
<span class="trg"><span class="trans">building </span></span>
#+end_example
In the Slovene one the translations are all in one paragraph, separated by middle dots:
#+begin_example
<p class="gloss">house · household, family</p>
#+end_example
In the Pali one the meaning is the first bold text:
#+begin_example
<div class=dpd><p>masc. <b>nature; character</b>
#+end_example


** Step 2. Write a spec
In =bltzread-dict-filters= each dictionary gets a spec. The key is the dictionary name or the language code. A spec is one of:

- A regexp string. Every match in the HTML gives one line: the text of its first group (or the whole match). For the Polish entry, this regexp means "the text right after class="trans">":
  #+begin_src emacs-lisp
  "class=\"trans\">\\([^<]*\\)"
  #+end_src
- =(:class "gloss")=: the text just after each element with that class. Shorter than a regexp when the class is all you need. Give a list for several classes.
- Extra keys for a list spec: =:split " · "= cuts each piece into separate lines, =:max 5= keeps the first five lines, =:join ", "= puts the lines on one line.
- A function taking the HTML, for anything else.

Tags and entities are removed, repeated lines dropped. Do not include the headword or the dictionary name: the card shows the word already.

** Step 3. My five dictionaries
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(setq bltzread-dict-filters
      '(("slv" :class "gloss" :split " · ")               ; DSSJ-E-sl-en
        ("pli" . "<div class=dpd><p>[^<]*<b>\\([^<]*\\)") ; dpd
        ("pol" . "class=\"trans\">\\([^<]*\\)")         ; Polish-English
        ("hrv" . "class=\"trans\">\\([^<]*\\)")         ; Croatian-English
        ("eng" :class "df" :max 5)))                      ; new-oxford-american-dictionary
#+end_src
Remember that inside a Lisp string every backslash is doubled.

** Step 4. Test it
Evaluate the =setq=, then rest on a word. If the card still shows the whole entry, your spec found nothing (bltzread then falls back to the full entry on purpose). Try the spec on its own with =M-x eval-expression=:
#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(bltzread-dict-apply-filter '(:class "gloss" :split " · ")
  "<p class=\"gloss\">house · family</p>")
#+end_src
This returns ="house\nfamily"=. Lookups are cached with the entry; run =M-x bltzread-dict-clear-cache= after changing a spec.

* If something goes wrong

- No colours :: Check =bltzr list-languages= and that the language code is right.
- No dictionary text :: Run =sdcv -l -2 YOUR-FOLDER= in a terminal and compare names with =bltzread-sdcv-dictionaries=.
- Keys do nothing :: The single keys work only in a read-only buffer. Another mode may also have taken them; use the =s= prefix, or rebind them.
- Words keep returning to level 1 :: You may be looking them up; each lookup restarts their count.
